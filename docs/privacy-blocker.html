Get past the privacy blocker

The first time you open MyWorkDock, macOS shows a warning and does not let you through. Here is why, and how to continue.

Why it happens

macOS Gatekeeper only opens apps that Apple has notarized. Notarizing needs a paid Apple Developer account, and MyWorkDock is a small independent app that is signed ad hoc instead. The app is not damaged and not flagged as malware: Apple simply has not reviewed it. The warning looks like one of these:

  • “MyWorkDock” Not Opened: Apple could not verify “MyWorkDock” is free of malware…
  • “MyWorkDock” can’t be opened because Apple cannot check it for malicious software.

You only need to approve it once.

Option 1: Open Anyway (recommended)

Works on macOS 15 Sequoia and later, and on macOS 14.

  1. Double-click MyWorkDock in Applications. When the warning appears, click Done or Cancel. Do not click Move to Trash.
  2. Open System Settings › Privacy & Security and scroll down to the Security section.
  3. You will see “MyWorkDock was blocked to protect your Mac.” Click Open Anyway.
  4. Enter your password or use Touch ID.
  5. Click Open Anyway once more in the final confirmation. The app starts.
No “Open Anyway” button?

It only appears for a while after a blocked launch. Double-click the app again, cancel the warning, and go back to Privacy & Security.

Option 2: right-click Open (macOS 14 only)

On Sonoma you can right-click the app, choose Open, then Open again. Apple removed this shortcut in macOS 15, so on Sequoia and later use Option 1.

Option 3: Terminal

If you prefer one command, remove the quarantine flag that the browser put on the download:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/MyWorkDock.app

Then open the app normally. Only do this for an app you downloaded from this project’s releases page.

What MyWorkDock does not ask for

No Accessibility, Input Monitoring, Screen Recording, Automation or Notifications permission. The global hotkeys use a macOS API that needs none of them. Everything you save (clips, todos, snippets) stays in files on your Mac, and the app has no analytics or tracking. Its only network activity of its own is the update check. See Updates.

Blocked again after an update?

Updates are installed by the app itself. If macOS ever blocks the app again afterwards, repeat Option 1.
